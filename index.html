Book One

Baseball Imposters: The Dark Side of Fandom

For over forty years, Rob Sheinkopf has tracked down the men who claimed they played Major League Baseball — and never did. Barflies, obituaries, old-timer's day crashers: men who built entire identities on a career that never happened.

"What W.P. Kinsella called the Eddie Scissons Syndrome, named for the fraudulent old pitcher in Shoeless Joe — the novel behind Field of Dreams."

This is investigative nonfiction with the pacing of a mystery: part sports history, part psychological case study of why ordinary men risk everything to claim a diamond they never stood on.

"Hey Mom, Wanna Have a Catch?"

The warmer companion to Baseball Imposters — 49 true, short stories about parents passing a love of the game on to their kids. No fabricated careers here, just the real thing: a ball, a glove, and a few minutes in the backyard.

Where Baseball Imposters asks why men lie about the game, this collection shows exactly what's worth telling the truth about.

About Rob

A retired higher-ed professional who never stopped chasing the truth about the game.

Rob Sheinkopf is a retired higher-education professional based in Syracuse, NY, and a lifelong student of baseball's stranger margins. For more than 40 years, he's collected stories of men who falsely claimed to have played Major League Baseball — a quiet obsession that eventually became Baseball Imposters: The Dark Side of Fandom.

He's an active member of the Society for American Baseball Research (SABR) and has presented his findings at the Cooperstown Symposium, held at the National Baseball Hall of Fame. Along the way, he also worked as a Luxury Suites Concierge for the San Francisco Giants — close enough to the real thing to spot a fake one coming.

  • SABR member & Cooperstown Symposium presenter
